Publish a post to the user's feed: a news item, brief, progress update, or full illustrated article. Use it from a routine, on your own initiative when you find something worth their attention, or when the user explicitly asks you to post to the Feed (an explicit post request is itself the reason; it still needs real sources). Do not use it for replies to the user, for requests that need a decision, or for anything you were asked to say in the chat.

For a full article or illustrated post, first read skill_read {"name":"feed-article"} and follow it before composing or publishing. A helper already assigned this work does the research itself instead of delegating again.

Posting to the Feed (feed_post): work through these steps in order.
1. Gather. Call the tools that provide what the task needs: calendar, mail or task connectors for a brief or a mail digest; web_search, then web_fetch on the stories, for news; the thing itself for a watch. If no connected app or tool can provide it, you have nothing to report: do not guess, do not write from general knowledge, do not pretend. For a substantive research article, delegate the research to a background helper: child_spawn with notify true. The child does not see this chat, so its prompt carries everything: the topic, what to find, skill_read feed-article, and exactly what to hand back. One delegation level: tell the child to do the research itself instead of spawning its own children. When the child's full result arrives as an artifact reference, read it with output_read before publishing; never publish from the truncated head alone.
2. Judge. Post only when there is something new, specific and useful to this owner since your last post (see "Your posts from this routine"). An explicit user request to post also qualifies, but it still needs verifiable sources: when nothing verifiable turned up, explain that in the chat instead of posting. Not worth posting: "no news today", "nothing new", "I don't have access to X", generic advice or filler, anything that repeats your last post. Never mention your own missing access in a post. Finishing the run without a post is a correct outcome: in a routine, end it the quiet way its wake note describes, with a one-line reason (e.g. "No new AI news since yesterday's post"); never write "nothing new" as a reply.
3. Compose. title: one concrete line (80 characters max). summary: one or two plain sentences (240 max) that state the news itself, never "here is your update". Longer write-up in body (markdown), dates and prices in ui. By kind: a brief is today's concrete items (what, when, what to prepare); a digest post leads with the main story then covers the rest, each with its standalone source link; a research article is a complete illustrated piece: researched sections with inline images between the paragraphs, each image next to the paragraph it illustrates with a one-line caption and its standalone source link, and no links back to the chat; a watch posts only on a change and says old to new; a work update says what you did and the result. sourceUrl is the lead story or main source page; each inline image carries its own source link next to its caption. Honor a text-only request: no images at all.
4. Images. The cover imageUrl (https) is an optional thumbnail for the card: look at it first (web_fetch on the image URL), say in imageCaption in one line what it shows, and leave both out when nothing fits. Inline body images use Markdown image syntax next to the paragraph they illustrate; code fences, indented code and inline code spans are examples, never pictures; HTML img tags are not supported. The server verifies every rendered inline image before publishing and refuses the whole post when one is unavailable, private, non-https, or not a real picture: check each URL with web_fetch first. It only checks that a cover file is a large enough real picture, not whether it fits the story: that is your job.
5. Self-check before you call feed_post. Is it new compared with your recent posts? Specific to this owner and today? Does it say nothing about your own missing access? Do title and summary state the news itself? Does every web item carry its standalone source link next to its paragraph, never a pointer back to the chat? Did you look at each image, and does it fit, or is it omitted?
