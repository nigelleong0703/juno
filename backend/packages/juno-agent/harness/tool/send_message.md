Tell the person something as its own message in the current conversation, or ask a question card. Always pass purpose, which says why you are sending it. In a run someone is waiting on, your final reply is delivered to this chat as one message, so use send_message there only for progress during long work, a question card, another chat, a private reply, or to send your reply as several messages (messages, then end your turn with no text). Never send the same thing twice and never narrate the delivery ("sent", "done"): an identical final reply is dropped. In a run nobody is waiting on (a routine, a watch, a scheduled check), your final text is not sent to anyone: this tool, or feed_post for longer news, is the only way to reach the person, and progress and answer are refused there.
Other rooms: conversationId (a room id only, such as one just created with conversation_create) sends there instead; targetAgentIds addresses specific agents in that room (the message stays visible with their names shown and wakes them). Rooms and targets take text only. toOwner true, in a group when your owner asks for something private: text is a one-line note (not the answer), posted in your direct chat with them, where you are woken with their original message and your final reply is the answer; say in this conversation only that you'll answer privately. It works only when your owner's own message started this run, and not in a chat that is already private. Write only what this recipient may know: e.g. say you're away 6–9 Nov, not where or why; never include health, money or private plans unless the person you help asked you to share them.
Cards: for a rich message (a comparison, a summary) pass ui next to a short lead-in text; text may be left out and then the bubble shows the card's text form. Use plain text for conversation and blocks only when there is structure: options, configurations, comparisons, approvals, final results. Blocks (shapes in the schema): text (a couple of sentences), facts, items (products or line items, with a total), table, group (related blocks as one titled section, e.g. one per option; display blocks only, no nesting), notice (the one-line consequence or caveat), link, image (a library image), and for questions choices or fields. Keep the title to one line, put details in facts, items or a table (no markdown tables or headers), and make the card stand on its own: the user may not have read the chat. Quick-reply buttons (a replies block) are real next steps the user may tap, never test or placeholder options.
Questions: kind question needs ui with exactly one interactive block: a choices block (pick one, or several with multiple) or a fields block (non-secret input: text, number, date, select). Gather everything only the user can answer into that one card (several answers: one fields block, a select field where there are set options; one pick: choices; yes/no or proceed/cancel: choices with two options); never ask across several turns what one card could ask. Put what the user is deciding (item, configuration, price, merchant, what happens next) in the ui itself, as facts, items or a notice. Write one or two short lines saying why in the same turn, then send the card: the card ends your turn, so you write nothing after it, and your lead-in lines are posted above it. The user's answer (the option label they tapped, "Label: value" lines for fields, or whatever they type instead) arrives as their next message and starts your next turn.
